# Reference video analysis: "In this town everybody huss"

All figures below were measured on the file itself (ffprobe, faster-whisper, Demucs voice/music separation, librosa, pyloudnorm). Nothing is estimated.

## Format
| | |
|---|---|
| Resolution | 1080×1920 (9:16) |
| Frame rate | 24 fps |
| Duration | 62.4 s |
| Video | H.264, ~2.9 Mb/s |
| Audio | AAC 48 kHz stereo, ~45 kb/s |
| Loudness | −14.2 LUFS, peak −3.0 dBFS |

## Narration (the heart of the video)
- **Who:** a single first-person narrator, a kid, who is the protagonist ("me").
- **Pitch:** high, a child's register. Median F0 is about 252 Hz, ranging from 177 to 376 Hz. That is a very wide range, so the delivery is animated and exaggerated.
- **Speed:** very fast, about 234 words per minute, with 243 words in 62 s. Speech starts at frame 1, with no intro and no title. Pauses are almost all under 0.5 s.
- **Tone:**
  - Deadpan comedy, playing it straight while describing absurd events.
  - It shifts into exaggerated drama for the effort ("Ahh… almost, almost, almost!").
  - It ends in triumph ("We won!").
- **Sound:**
  - Dry: no reverb, close mic, strongly compressed.
  - The voice alone sits at −14.2 LUFS, which is the same loudness as the whole mix. It dominates completely.
- **Catchphrase:** a made-up sound word ("HUSS"), repeated as a running gag. It is written in caps with "!" when shouted.

## Music
- **There is no background music.** After separating voice from everything else, the non-voice track sits at −58 LUFS (effectively silence) for the first ~35 s.
- After 35 s, the non-voice track holds only crowd and stadium noise and the crowd chanting the catchphrase, at about −27 to −30 LUFS. These are noisy, wideband sounds, not tonal music.
- The beat tracker reports ~143 BPM, but that is only the rhythm of the chant. There is no clear key; the chroma is spread out.
- **What this means for the new video:** voice first, no music, and sparse sound effects that only build to a crowd roar at the climax.

## Story structure (the formula)
1. **Hook in the first 2 s:** "In this town, everybody ___. Except me."
2. Backstory: "I used to ___ the most."
3. Disaster: the ability is gone.
4. A doctor's diagnosis with a funny rule: "Too much ___. No ___ for a month."
5. Deadline: "The big game was in one week."
6. A list of absurd remedies that fail, each in a short punchy sentence.
7. Humiliation: even the dog does it better.
8. A friend invites him to a party with a backhanded insult. He closes the door.
9. A training montage at night, in silence. The town gets an unexpected benefit.
10. The big game. The rival team is ridiculously strong.
11. The clock: 10 seconds left, down by two.
12. A failed attempt ("almost… almost…"), and the rivals laugh.
13. The saved-up power returns, with absurd over-the-top consequences (net rips, scoreboard falls, ref's whistle flies).
14. "We won!"
15. A callback ending that returns to the hook.

## Visual style
- **Look:**
  - Original blocky Roblox-style avatars in a clean, bright, low-poly world.
  - Saturated pastel colours.
  - A sky-blue sky with soft white puffy clouds.
  - Soft, almost flat lighting with soft shadows. No bloom, no film grain, no vignette.
- **Places:** a suburban town of pastel houses with gabled roofs, grass, grey asphalt roads with white lines, street lamps, a hospital room, a living room, a bathroom, a night street, a party house, and an outdoor stadium with a scoreboard.
- **Hero:**
  - A blue beanie with a yellow letter.
  - A blue hoodie with a big yellow word and little white stars.
  - Simple drawn faces: dot or line eyes and expressive mouths.
  - Very big reactions: wide eyes, an "O" mouth, a halo for the "angel" gag.
- **Cutting:** 67 cuts in 62 s, about one cut every 0.93 s. The angles change a lot:
  - aerial establishing shots
  - extreme close-ups on the face
  - point-of-view shots and over-the-shoulder shots
  - low angles for heroic moments
  - insert shots of props
- **Animation:** simple, readable poses with little camera movement inside a shot. The energy comes from the cuts.
- **Captions:**
  - One word at a time, in sync with the voice.
  - White rounded bold sans-serif, about 65 px, with a thin black outline and a soft shadow.
  - Centred at about 58% of the height. No pop animation.
  - Shouted words are in caps ("HUSS!").

## What we keep and what we change
- **Kept, because it is style:**
  - the format, pace and cutting rhythm
  - a fast, high, funny kid narrator
  - no music, sparse sound effects and a crowd climax
  - the bright flat blocky look and the plain white one-word captions
  - the story formula
- **Changed, because it must be original:**
  - a new story, catchphrase, characters, outfit, places and sport
  - all lines written from scratch
  - the voice is synthetic and is **not** cloned from the reference narrator
